\section*{S2. External transfer-matrix comparisons}
\subsection*{S2.1. Classical AlN/BaTiO$_3$ bilayer}
For the classical bilayer, $a_A=a_B=0.01\,\mathrm{m}$ and the period is $b=0.02\,\mathrm{m}$. Its transfer-matrix trace reduces to the Rytov relation in the main text \cite{li2024anchorB,zhengwei2009}. The homogeneous single-layer identity and identical-material bilayer reduction have maximum relative residuals of $6.47\times10^{-16}$ and $7.22\times10^{-16}$. The heterogeneous trace agrees with the Rytov relation to $2.22\times10^{-16}$. The calculated normalized stop-band intervals are $[0.4806,0.5209]$, $[0.9812,1.0215]$, and $[1.4993,1.5028]$. We compare with the published plot visually because numerical source curves are unavailable; no percentage error is inferred from raster plots.

\subsection*{S2.2. Gradient-elastic bilayers and validation limits}
In the AlN/BaTiO$_3$ gradient case, the published plot lists $l=10^{-5}$ and $l_1=2\times10^{-5}$. The surrounding text gives a macroscale layer width, but the plot does not say whether the length symbols are dimensional or normalized. The paper defines $\bar l=l/b$ with $b=a_A+a_B=0.02\,\mathrm{m}$. Reading the printed $10^{-5}$ as $\bar l$ gives $l=2\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m}$; using a single layer width as the scale gives $10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m}$. Together with the dimensional-micro and dimensional-macroscale readings, these choices produce different transfer-matrix curves. For the dimensional-micro interpretation, the calculated stop bands are $[0.078,0.242]$, $[0.378,0.383]$, $[0.742,0.760]$, $[0.785,1.103]$, and $[1.123,1.435]$. The homogeneous-limit and identical-material reductions each have an algebraic residual of $4.10\times10^{-57}$. These internal identities do not establish agreement with the publication. We do not treat this case as \emph{externally validated}: the source convention is unresolved and numerical source data are unavailable \cite{li2024anchorB}.

For the Pb/brass shear-horizontal case, the evaluated ratios are $c_1/a_1^2=0.15$, $d_1/a_1=0.25$, and $c_2/c_1=d_2/d_1=1.5$. Li et al. take $c_1$ and $d_1$ from a shared numerical example; the cited comparison panel does not specify them. The calculated stop-band intervals are $[0.340,1.024]$, $[1.423,1.867]$, and $[2.477,2.911]$. The homogeneous-limit identity and identical-material reduction have relative residuals of $1.37\times10^{-13}$ and $4.19\times10^{-14}$, respectively. The implementation reproduces the printed dipolar layer-matrix formulation to machine precision. This checks the formulation, not the band curves. We do not treat those curves as \emph{externally validated} because the cited panel omits its parameter set, normalization, and numerical data \cite{li2023anchorA,liweizhou2016}.

\subsection*{S2.3. Pure-bending micro-beam limit}
The pure-bending micro-beam reduction matches the closed-form relations of Papargyri-Beskou and Beskos to better than $10^{-15}$ in both evaluated limits \cite{papargyribeskou2009}. This analytical benchmark is separate from the plot-based bilayer comparisons.

\section*{S4. Case-C gap resolution and parameter sensitivity}
Table~\ref{tab:s4_caseC_mesh} separates the effects of finite-element mesh and Brillouin-zone sampling on the Case-C complete gap. At the $4\times4$ mesh, the four leg/path values are $\Delta[\Gamma\text{--}X]=2.6114$, $\Delta[X\text{--}M]=2.7563$, $\Delta[M\text{--}\Gamma]=3.2871$, and $\Delta[\mathrm{path}]=2.5732$, compared with $\Delta[\mathrm{complete}]=2.5732$. On the coarsest mesh, the $X$-point gap exceeds the complete gap because the minimum upper-band frequency lies off $X$ along the $\Gamma$--$M$ diagonal. From $8\times8$ onward, the reported $X$-point and complete gaps coincide to the shown precision.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Case-C complete-gap and $X$-point gap values under mesh refinement.}

\input{supplementary/tables/tabS4_caseC_resolution.tex}

At the $4\times4$ mesh, increasing the Brillouin-zone grid from $11\times11$ to $21\times21$ leaves the complete gap at $2.5732$. The $41\times41$ grid gives $2.5728$. As the minimum along $\Gamma$--$M$ is better resolved, the upper edge shifts from $7.1430$ to $7.1426$. For $8\times8$ and $16\times16$ meshes, the $11\times11$ and $21\times21$ grids agree to four decimals; for $32\times32$ and $64\times64$, they agree to six decimals. With the FE mesh fixed at $4\times4$, using $4\times4$, $6\times6$, and $8\times8$ Gauss points per cut element gives $X$-point gaps of $2.7563$, $2.7226$, and $2.7862$, respectively (changes of $-1.22\%$ and $+1.08\%$ relative to the $4\times4$ rule). Under radius variation, $r_0/L=0.20$--$0.40$ corresponds to filling fractions $12.6\%$--$50.3\%$. The complete gap changes from $0.8288$ ($16.61\%$) at $r_0/L=0.20$ to $2.5732$ ($43.94\%$) at $0.30$, then to $2.4426$ ($37.00\%$) at $0.40$. The sequence in Table~\ref{tab:s4_caseC_mesh} decreases with mesh refinement; the quoted coarse-mesh maximum is not a converged continuum value.
