% Abstract
%-------------------------------------------------
\begin{abstract}
Periodic microstructured solids can exhibit direction-dependent dispersion when the wavelength approaches an internal material length. Here, a rotated ellipsoidal characteristic-length tensor is coupled to local Mindlin Form-II strain-gradient elasticity and an independent gradient micro-inertia. A two-dimensional Bloch--Floquet formulation is discretized with a conforming 32-degree-of-freedom Bogner--Fox--Schmit element, applying the same complex Bloch phase to displacement and derivative degrees of freedom. In the homogeneous medium, changing microstructural orientation and aspect ratio redistributes the branches and produces directional stop bands, while no complete gap is found ($\Delta_{\mathrm{complete}}\le -0.3235$). The largest sampled directional gap is $\Delta_{GX}=0.0677$ at $\theta=0^\circ$, $\mathrm{AR}=7$; the orientation sensitivity reaches $S_\theta=2.34\,\mathrm{rad}^{-1}$ at $\mathrm{AR}=10$. On the acoustic branch at $\bar{k}=0.5$, the maximum group-velocity deviation is $2.78^\circ$ for $\mathrm{AR}=10$; over the sampled orientation grid, orientation repositions the steering lobes while the peak deviation remains invariant within $10^{-5}\,$deg. A separate high-contrast phononic-crystal case exhibits a positive complete gap of $2.5732$ ($43.94\%$) on the $4\times4$ mesh, but the gap decreases to $1.9179$ at $64\times64$ and is not mesh-converged. Analytical and numerical results further show that gradient micro-inertia bounds the high-wavenumber phase speed. These findings distinguish orientation-based directional control in a homogeneous medium from complete attenuation enabled by material contrast.
\end{abstract}
