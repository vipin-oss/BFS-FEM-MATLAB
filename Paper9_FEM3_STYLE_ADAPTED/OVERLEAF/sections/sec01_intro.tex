\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Periodic elastic media provide a route to controlling wave propagation through their dispersion structure. Classical calculations established band structures for periodic composites, and experiments and theory demonstrated absolute acoustic gaps in two-dimensional solids \cite{SigalasEconomou1992,kushwaha1993,vasseur2001}. Subsequent lattice analyses and topology-optimization studies showed how geometry can be used to tailor pass bands, stop bands, and wave transmission \cite{MartinssonMovchan2003,SigmundJensen2003}. The broader foundations, computational methods, and applications of phononic materials are reviewed in the phononics literature \cite{Hussein2014phononics,maldovan2013,khelifadibi2015}. These results motivate a distinction central to the present work: periodicity can suppress propagation over selected directions, while a complete gap requires suppression over the full Brillouin zone.

When wavelengths approach a material's microstructural scale, a local Cauchy law may not represent size-dependent stiffness or dispersion. Higher-order theories introduce additional kinematic measures and their work-conjugate stresses. Their foundations include couple-stress and microstructure theories, second-gradient elasticity, and variational descriptions of higher-order boundary work \cite{toupin1962,MindlinTiersten1962,mindlin1964,Mindlin1965,mindlineshel1968,Germain1973}. Related size-effect research spans strain-gradient descriptions of nonuniform deformation and small-scale beam experiments \cite{Aifantis1999,FleckHutchinson1993,FleckHutchinson1997,YangChongLamTong2002,LamYangChongWangTong2003}. Unified, implicit, and finite-element formulations have since broadened the modelling and computational options for gradient elasticity \cite{AskesAifantis2002,askesaifantis2011,AskesAifantis2006,AskesGutierrez2006,AskesMorataAifantis2008,polyzosfotiadis2012}. The constitutive model used here is the local Mindlin Form-II strain-gradient theory; nonlocal integral and micromorphic theories are related, but distinct, formulations.

A dynamic gradient model requires an explicit kinetic-energy law as well as gradient stiffness. With classical inertia alone, the shear branch considered here has phase speed proportional to wavenumber at high wavenumber; the independent gradient micro-inertia regularizes that branch to a finite limit. This conclusion is specific to the present constitutive and kinetic-energy choices, not a universal statement about all higher-order continua. One-dimensional studies have examined dispersion in gradient-elastic beams and periodic media, including dipolar-gradient formulations \cite{papargyribeskou2009,liweizhou2016,HosseiniSladekSladekZhang2024}. A two-dimensional phononic crystal has also been treated with a relaxed micromorphic model containing free and gradient micro-inertia \cite{MadeoColletMiniaciBillonOuisseNeff2018}. These studies establish the importance of the continuum and inertia law when interpreting short-wavelength bands, while retaining constitutive distinctions from the local Mindlin Form-II model considered here.

Size-dependent phononic-crystal research now spans several dimensions and constitutive settings. Classical transfer-matrix analyses show that imperfect interfaces can alter one-dimensional band gaps \cite{zhengwei2009}; other one-dimensional studies consider dipolar-gradient elasticity, gradient thermoelasticity, and flexoelectric strain-gradient coupling \cite{liweizhou2016,li2023anchorA,li2024anchorB,HosseiniSladekSladekZhang2024}. In two dimensions, nonlocal strain-gradient theory has been used for nanoscale phononic crystals, and related work has examined mixed and defect modes of a phononic-crystal slab \cite{JinHuHu2022,JinHuHu2023}. Thermoelastic cylindrical crystals and functionally graded lattices provide further, model-specific points of comparison \cite{hosseinizhang2021,mishra2026anchorC}. Anisotropy and orientation effects are also established in conventional phononic crystals: studies of orthotropic fillers and anisotropic plates report changes in band locations and widths as material axes or inclusions are rotated relative to the lattice \cite{zhanwei2010,YaoWuHouXin2011}. Thus, orientation-dependent band tuning itself is not new. The narrower question addressed here is how an oriented characteristic-length tensor affects dispersion and energy-flow direction within a local two-dimensional strain-gradient model with gradient micro-inertia.

The displacement-based numerical treatment is also consequential. A strain-gradient weak form containing second displacement derivatives requires an $H^2$-conforming approximation or an equivalent mixed formulation. The bicubic Bogner--Fox--Schmit (BFS) rectangle supplies $C^1$ inter-element continuity on structured quadrilateral meshes \cite{bfs1965}. Earlier work developed displacement-based, mixed, and implicit-gradient finite elements \cite{ShuKingFleck1999,AmanatidouAravas2002,ZervosPapanastasiouVardoulakis2001,ZervosPapanicolopulosVardoulakis2009,Beheshti2017}; subsequent studies addressed staggered and recovered-gradient schemes, higher-order Hermite mappings, distorted domains, and mixed elements with shared degrees of freedom \cite{AskesAifantis2006,AskesGutierrez2006,AskesMorataAifantis2008,AndreausDellIsolaGiorgioPlacidiLekszyckiRizzi2016,ChoiLeeSim2023,Papanicolopulos2024,ChirkovNazarenkoAltenbach2024,BacciocchiFantuzzi2023,BacciocchiFantuzzi2025}. In a conforming BFS Bloch element, the phase transformation must be applied consistently to the displacement values and derivative degrees of freedom, including the mixed derivative.

To the best of our knowledge, the studies cited above do not combine, in one two-dimensional Bloch--Floquet finite-element framework, a rotated ellipsoidal second-moment characteristic-length tensor, local Mindlin Form-II strain-gradient elasticity with independent gradient micro-inertia, and a conforming $C^1$ BFS discretization with phase transformations on all relevant degrees of freedom. The gap is this specific combination, not a general absence of anisotropy, orientation effects, or size-dependent phononic-crystal research. The present study makes four contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{C\arabic*.}]
  \item A Bloch--Floquet framework is formulated for two-dimensional anisotropic strain-gradient elasticity, with the length tensor derived from a rotated ellipsoidal averaging domain so that aspect ratio $\mathrm{AR}=l_1/l_2$ and orientation $\theta$ are distinct design variables.
  \item The role of gradient micro-inertia is examined analytically and numerically. For the constitutive branch studied here, $\ell_{\mathrm{i}}=0$ gives an unbounded high-wavenumber phase-speed branch, whereas $\ell_{\mathrm{i}}>0$ yields the finite limit $v_{T,\infty}=\sqrt{\mu/\rho}\,l_{\mathrm{eff}}/(\sqrt{10}\,\ell_{\mathrm{i}})$ derived in Appendix~\ref{app:asymptotics}.
  \item A conforming 32-degree-of-freedom BFS element is developed, with complex Bloch phases applied consistently to displacement, first derivatives, and the mixed derivative.
  \item Directional dispersion, gap measures, group velocity, and energy flux are evaluated over the $(\theta,\mathrm{AR})$ design space. The analysis distinguishes changes in directional stop bands from complete gaps and quantifies both steering amplitude and lobe orientation.
\end{enumerate}
