\subsection{Anisotropy reshapes the homogeneous spectrum}
Figure~\ref{fig:caseH_dispersion} compares the isotropic reference with two orientations of the anisotropic length tensor. Both acoustic branches start at zero frequency at $\Gamma$. Aspect ratio and orientation shift the branches and lift symmetry-related degeneracies at the zone edge. At $\mathrm{AR}=10$ and $\theta=45^\circ$, a narrow stop band appears along $\Gamma$--$X$, with $\Delta_{GX}=0.0043$. Modes propagating in other directions overlap this interval, so it is not a complete gap; the full-zone measure remains $\Delta_{\mathrm{complete}}\le -0.3235$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig06_caseH_dispersion.pdf}
\caption{Case-H Bloch bands along $\Gamma$--$X$--$M$--$\Gamma$. (a) Isotropic reference ($\mathrm{AR}=1$) and aligned anisotropy ($\mathrm{AR}=10$, $\theta=0^\circ$). (b) Isotropic reference and diagonal anisotropy ($\mathrm{AR}=10$, $\theta=45^\circ$), which opens a $\Gamma$--$X$ stop band but no complete gap.}
\label{fig:caseH_dispersion}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:theta_sweep} shows the zone-edge frequencies as the tensor rotates. At $\mathrm{AR}=5$, as $\theta$ changes from $0^\circ$ to $90^\circ$, the transverse and longitudinal frequencies at $X$ fall from $2.915$ to $2.672$ and from $5.050$ to $4.628$, respectively, as the projected length scale shifts from the major to the minor axis. The $M$-point frequencies are symmetric about $45^\circ$, as expected from the square-cell symmetry.

Figure~\ref{fig:ar_sweep} shows the effect of elongation at $\theta=45^\circ$. The isotropic reference already has an orientation-independent directional width $\Delta_{GX}=0.041$. As $\mathrm{AR}$ increases, the branches shift relative to one another, and the directional gap varies non-monotonically.

\subsection{Periodic material contrast opens a complete gap}
\label{sec:caseC_pc}
Case~C combines gradient elasticity with a periodic epoxy--YBCO impedance contrast ($\chi_\mu=25.0$, $\chi_\rho=5.546$; Table~\ref{tab:case_params}), unlike homogeneous Case~H. We denote the cell period by $L$ in the text and $a$ in Figure~\ref{fig:caseC_dispersion}; $r_0/L=r_0/a=0.30$ gives a filling fraction of $28.27\%$. Figure~\ref{fig:caseC_dispersion} shows a complete gap between Bands~3 and~4 over $\bar{\omega}\in[4.5698,7.1430]$. On the $4\times4$ mesh and $21\times21$ Brillouin-zone grid, $\Delta_{\mathrm{complete}}=2.5732$, or $43.94\%$. We integrate the circular interface by sub-cell Gauss sampling on the Cartesian mesh. Brillouin-zone refinement changes the quoted width by less than $4\times10^{-4}$; finite-element refinement has a much larger effect.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig07_caseC_dispersion.pdf}
\caption{Case-C complete-gap formation and radius dependence. (a) Bands along $\Gamma$--$X$--$M$--$\Gamma$ for $r_0/L=0.30$, with the $4\times4$-mesh gap between Bands~3 and~4. (b) Complete-gap width versus $r_0/a$ at the same mesh; the $r_0/a=0.30$ result is not mesh-converged.}
\label{fig:caseC_dispersion}
\end{figure}

Under mesh refinement, the complete gap falls from $2.5732$ at $4\times4$ to $2.2504$, $2.0722$, $1.9736$, and $1.9179$ at $8\times8$, $16\times16$, $32\times32$, and $64\times64$, respectively. It stays open at every computed resolution but has not converged. The value $2.5732$ is therefore a coarse-mesh result, not a continuum estimate. At the $4\times4$ mesh, increasing $r_0/L$ from $0.20$ to $0.30$ widens the gap from $0.8288$ ($16.61\%$) to $2.5732$ ($43.94\%$); it then narrows to $2.4426$ ($37.00\%$) at $r_0/L=0.40$. Supplementary Material~S4 gives the mesh, quadrature, and zone-sampling details.
