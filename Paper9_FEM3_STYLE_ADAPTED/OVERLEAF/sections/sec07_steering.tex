\section{Wave steering and micro-inertia}
\label{sec:steering}

Orientation redistributes the bands, and we use group velocity to track the associated energy-flow direction. We also examine gradient-energy participation and the high-wavenumber limit for the transverse branch.

\subsection{Group-velocity steering}
On the constant-wavenumber ring $|\bmk|=\bar{k}=0.5$, anisotropy makes the group velocity non-collinear with the wave vector. Figure~\ref{fig:ifc_wave_steering} plots the angular deviation $\delta(\phi)=\angle\bmv_g-\angle\bmk$ and group-velocity magnitude at $\theta=45^\circ$. The peak deviation rises from $0^\circ$ at $\mathrm{AR}=1$ to $1.4146^\circ$ at $\mathrm{AR}=5$ and $2.7846^\circ$ at $\mathrm{AR}=10$. The magnitude $|\bmv_g|$ also varies around the contour, so orientation changes the energy-transport speed as well as its direction.

\subsection{Orientation repositions the steering lobes}
Figure~\ref{fig:s7_steering_sweep} and Table~\ref{tab:steering_sweep} report the orientation sweep $\theta\in\{0^\circ,15^\circ,\ldots,90^\circ\}$ for $\mathrm{AR}=5$ and $10$ on the same ring and acoustic branch. Across the sampled orientations, the peak deviation remains $1.4146^\circ$ and $2.7846^\circ$, respectively, to within $10^{-5}\,$deg. The principal direction follows $\phi^*(\theta)\simeq(45^\circ-\theta)\bmod90^\circ$; at $\mathrm{AR}=10$, its excursions are at most $5^\circ$. The grid therefore shows the steering lobes shifting with orientation while the peak amplitude remains nearly unchanged.

We define the sampled steering measure as follows:
\begin{equation}
M_s(\mathrm{AR})=\phi^*(90^\circ,\mathrm{AR})-\phi^*(0^\circ,\mathrm{AR}),
\label{eq:Ms_def}
\end{equation}
For the two aspect ratios, the values are:
\begin{equation}
M_s(\mathrm{AR}=5)=0^\circ,\qquad M_s(\mathrm{AR}=10)=10^\circ.
\label{eq:Ms_value}
\end{equation}
The $5^\circ$ ring-sampling step sets the resolution of this difference. The deviation field is headless-periodic and mirror co-symmetric, with residuals no greater than $1.3\times10^{-6}\,$deg. The peak amplitude is invariant under $\theta\mapsto90^\circ-\theta$ to within $10^{-7}\,$deg. These symmetries are consistent with orientation shifting the lobes while leaving the sampled peak deviation unchanged.

\subsection{Gradient energy and the high-wavenumber limit}
For Mindlin Form-II elasticity, the strain energy splits into classical and gradient parts, $W=W_c+W_g$, with $W_c=\tfrac12\int_\Omega\sigma_{ij}\varepsilon_{ij}\,\dif\Omega$ and $W_g=\tfrac12\int_\Omega\tau_{ijk}\eta_{ijk}\,\dif\Omega$. Along $\Gamma$--$X$, the gradient-energy fraction rises from $0.20\%$ at $\bar{k}=0.1$ to $16.49\%$ at $\bar{k}=1.0$ (Figure~\ref{fig:energy_microinertia}). Double-stress work therefore contributes more as the wavelength approaches the cell scale.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig13_energy_microinertia.pdf}
\caption{(a) Gradient-energy fraction $W_g/W$ versus wavenumber. (b) Transverse-branch phase velocity with and without gradient micro-inertia; the finite high-wavenumber limit is specific to the shear branch analyzed here.}
\label{fig:energy_microinertia}
\end{figure}

The transverse shear branch analyzed in Appendix~\ref{app:asymptotics} has the asymptotic limit
\begin{equation}
\bar{v}_{T,\infty}=\frac{\bar{l}_{\mathrm{eff}}}{\sqrt{10}\,\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{i}}}=\frac{0.20}{\sqrt{10}\times0.20}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{10}}\approx0.3162.
\label{eq:v_horizon_sec7}
\end{equation}
At $\bar{k}=200$, the computed phase speed with micro-inertia is $0.3163$, within $0.03\%$ of this asymptote. Without micro-inertia, the analytical branch relation grows without bound, and the computed phase speed reaches $39.75$ at $\bar{k}=200$. This result applies only to the transverse branch and constitutive model studied; it does not establish a speed bound for other branches or models.
