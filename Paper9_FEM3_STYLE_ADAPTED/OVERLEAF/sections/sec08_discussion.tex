\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results show two distinct roles for microstructure. In the homogeneous Case~H calculations, rotating and elongating the characteristic-length tensor changes branch positions, directional gap widths, and the group-velocity direction, but the complete-gap measure remains negative. Within the sampled steering grid, orientation moves the principal steering direction while leaving the peak deviation nearly unchanged. These findings identify orientation as a control for directional dispersion and energy flow, not as evidence of complete attenuation in a homogeneous medium.

The Case~C calculation illustrates a different mechanism: periodic impedance contrast produces a positive complete gap. Its reported width, however, falls from $2.5732$ on a $4\times4$ mesh to $1.9179$ on a $64\times64$ mesh and has not converged. The coarse-mesh value should therefore be interpreted as a resolution-specific result; further refinement is needed before assigning a converged gap width. The Case-H and Case-C gap values also describe different material and discretization settings and should not be compared as if they were one controlled parameter sweep.

The external bilayer checks have unequal evidential status. The classical AlN/BaTiO$_3$ calculation is checked against the Rytov relation and compared graphically with the published plot. The two gradient-elastic examples are reproductions only, not external validations, because source parameter conventions or numerical comparison data are insufficiently specified (Supplementary Material~S2).

The model is linear, local, isothermal, and two-dimensional under plane-strain assumptions. Nonlinear gradient effects, dissipation, piezoelectric coupling, and three-dimensional polarization modes are outside the present analysis. The finite high-wavenumber phase-speed result is confined to the transverse branch and constitutive model examined here; it should not be generalized beyond that setting.
