\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

In homogeneous Case~H, the rotated characteristic-length tensor changes the gradient-stiffness contribution with propagation direction. Rotating or elongating the tensor therefore shifts some branches more than others: adjacent band edges can separate along $\Gamma$--$X$ even while modes at other wave vectors remain in the same frequency interval. This explains why Case~H supports a directional stop band but has no complete gap. Its design role is direction-selective filtering rather than omnidirectional attenuation. The sampled design map is non-monotone, so it can identify candidate configurations but does not establish a continuous optimum.

The steering results follow from the same direction-dependent dispersion. Anisotropy changes the shape of the acoustic dispersion surface, making group velocity non-collinear with the wave vector; rotating the length tensor then repositions the principal steering direction. Over the sampled orientations, the peak deviation is nearly unchanged. Thus orientation primarily redirects the energy-flow lobe in this calculation, rather than increasing its sampled peak amplitude. The reported steering remains specific to the acoustic branch and $\bar{k}=0.5$ ring.

Case~C adds periodic contrast in shear modulus and density. The resulting spatial variation in stiffness and inertia separates Bands~3 and~4 over the sampled Brillouin zone, producing a positive complete-gap measure. This differs from Case~H, where the tensor shifts branches selectively and other directions retain overlapping modes. The Case-C gap decreases from $2.5732$ on the $4\times4$ mesh to $1.9179$ on the $64\times64$ mesh and is not converged. Case~H and Case~C also use different material and discretization settings; their gap values are not a controlled parameter sweep, and the coarse-mesh Case-C width is not a continuum estimate.

The homogeneous mesh study gives an empirical rate $p=4.17$ over the $4^2$, $8^2$, and $16^2$ levels. The $32^2$ result is resolution-limited and excluded from the fit, so this rate is not a theoretical-order claim. Seven of the eight tabulated consistency checks pass. The energy-flux/group-velocity identity remains marked for recheck after the harmonic micro-inertia sign correction because the raw fields needed to rerun it are unavailable.

The classical AlN/BaTiO$_3$ calculation reproduces the Rytov transfer-matrix relation to $2.22\times10^{-16}$; comparison with the published B1 curves is visual only because tabulated source values are unavailable. The two gradient-elastic bilayer cases are reproductions, not quantitative external validations: source parameter conventions are unresolved and the numerical curves needed for comparison are unavailable. No quantitative validation claim is made for those heterogeneous benchmarks.

The analysis is linear, local, isothermal, and two-dimensional under plane-strain assumptions. It does not include nonlinear gradient effects, dissipation, piezoelectric coupling, or three-dimensional polarization modes. The finite high-wavenumber phase-speed result applies only to the transverse branch and constitutive model examined here.
