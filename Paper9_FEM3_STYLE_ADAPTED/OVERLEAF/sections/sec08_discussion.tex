\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

In homogeneous Case~H, the rotated characteristic-length tensor changes the gradient-stiffness contribution according to the wave-vector direction relative to its principal axes. The resulting branch shifts are unequal: rotating the tensor can separate neighboring bands along $\Gamma$--$X$ while modes at other wave vectors remain in the same frequency interval. Under the volume-equivalent scaling, changing the aspect ratio lengthens one principal characteristic length while shortening the other, so the two bounding band edges need not move together. Their frequency difference can therefore vary non-monotonically. A positive $\Delta_{GX}$ is a stop band only along $\Gamma$--$X$; modes elsewhere in the Brillouin zone remain consistent with the negative complete-gap measure $\Delta_{\mathrm{complete}}\le -0.3235$. Thus, the homogeneous design supports direction-selective filtering, not full-zone attenuation. The 42-point map can compare evaluated configurations but does not identify a continuous optimum.

The group-velocity response follows from the same direction-dependent dispersion. Because $\bmv_g=\nabla_{\bmk}\omega$ is normal to a local constant-frequency contour, changes in contour slope alter the angle between the energy-flow direction and the wave vector. Rotating the length tensor reorients these local contour normals and shifts the steering lobes. On the sampled acoustic branch at $\bar{k}=0.5$, the peak deviation is nearly unchanged while its principal direction moves. Within this calculation, orientation therefore redirects the energy flow without materially increasing its peak angular deviation; this interpretation is limited to the branch, ring, and sampled orientations.

Case~C adds periodic contrast in both shear modulus and density. The resulting Bloch bands separate Bands~3 and~4 over the sampled Brillouin zone, unlike the path-specific branch separation in homogeneous Case~H. This difference is consistent with the positive complete-gap measure in Case~C and the absence of a complete gap in Case~H. The Case-C gap decreases from $2.5732$ on the $4\times4$ mesh to $1.9179$ on the $64\times64$ mesh and remains unconverged. Because the cases use different material and discretization settings, the comparison is interpretive rather than a controlled parameter sweep; the coarse-mesh Case-C width is not a continuum estimate.

The homogeneous mesh study gives an empirical rate $p=4.17$ over the $4^2$, $8^2$, and $16^2$ levels. The $32^2$ result is resolution-limited and excluded from the fit, so this rate is not a theoretical-order claim. Seven of the eight tabulated consistency checks pass. After the harmonic micro-inertia sign correction in Appendix~\ref{app:energy_flux}, the energy-flux/group-velocity identity remains unresolved because the raw harmonic fields needed for an independent recalculation are unavailable.

The classical AlN/BaTiO$_3$ calculation reproduces the Rytov transfer-matrix relation to $2.22\times10^{-16}$; comparison with the published B1 curves is visual only because tabulated source values are unavailable. The two gradient-elastic bilayer cases are reproductions, not quantitative external validations: source parameter conventions are unresolved and the numerical curves needed for comparison are unavailable. No quantitative validation claim is made for those heterogeneous benchmarks.

For the transverse branch in Appendix~\ref{app:asymptotics}, gradient stiffness raises the phase speed at high wavenumber when micro-inertia is absent. With micro-inertia, the $k^2$ contribution in the numerator of $v_p^2$ is balanced asymptotically by the $k^2$ inertial term in its denominator, leaving a finite limit set by the ratio of the gradient-stiffness and micro-inertia lengths. This is a mechanism-specific result for the branch and constitutive model studied, not a universal bound. More broadly, the model is linear, local, isothermal, and two-dimensional under plane-strain assumptions; it excludes nonlinear gradient effects, dissipation, piezoelectric coupling, and three-dimensional polarization modes.
