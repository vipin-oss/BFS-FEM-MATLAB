\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We applied a two-dimensional Bloch--FEM formulation to local Mindlin Form-II strain-gradient elasticity with an oriented characteristic-length tensor and gradient micro-inertia. The consistency table lists seven checks as passing and flags the energy-flux identity for recheck after the Appendix B sign correction. The observed Case-H mesh rate is $p=4.17$ over the $4^2$, $8^2$, and $16^2$ meshes; the $32^2$ result is resolution-limited and excluded from the fit.

In homogeneous Case~H, orientation tunes directional gaps but does not produce a complete gap. The largest sampled directional width is $\Delta_{GX}=0.0677$ at $\theta=0^\circ$, $\mathrm{AR}=7$, while $\Delta_{\mathrm{complete}}\le-0.3235$. At $\bar{k}=0.5$, the acoustic-branch steering deviation reaches $2.7846^\circ$ for $\mathrm{AR}=10$. Across the sampled orientations, the steering lobes move while their peak deviation remains invariant within $10^{-5}\,$deg. In the separate high-contrast Case~C calculation, the complete gap is $2.5732$ ($43.94\%$) on the $4\times4$ mesh and falls to $1.9179$ at $64\times64$; it has not converged.

For the transverse branch studied, gradient micro-inertia bounds the high-wavenumber phase speed at the predicted value $\bar{v}_{T,\infty}=0.3162$. Orientation therefore tunes directional dispersion and steering, while the Case~C complete-gap estimate remains sensitive to mesh refinement.
