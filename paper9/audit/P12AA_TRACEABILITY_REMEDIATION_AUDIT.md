## Part D — P12AA-O3: TV6 / TV14 / TV18 adjudication

Three CSV-vs-JSON state-word divergences were inherited from P12Z-O3. Each was traced to the
underlying claim and its evidence:

* **TV6** — CSV `PARTIAL [S]` vs JSON `LOCKED [S]`. This is a **genuine inconsistency**, not a
  vocabulary variant: the CSV text still said "Case C inclusion parameters remain OPEN", while the
  register already carries a dedicated closed sub-claim (`TV6-CaseC`, `CLOSED [S]`, drawing on the
  published Zhan & Wei (2010) parameters), and the authoritative JSON records `LOCKED [S]`. The two
  files asserted different governed states for the same claim. **Corrected** byte-minimally: the
  status word aligned to `LOCKED [S]` and the stale clause reworded to
  "were OPEN at P5 and are closed in P11 (see TV6-CaseC)". The parameter text itself is untouched.
* **TV14 / TV18** — CSV `CLOSED [S]` vs JSON `LOCKED [S]`. Identical provenance class `[S]` and
  identical governed meaning; both words are accepted by the repository's own resolution predicate
  (`check_traceability.py` treats `CLOSED`/`LOCKED`/`RESOLVED`/`DISCHARGED` alike), and no rule
  distinguishes them. Classified as a **harmless equivalent** and **left unnormalised** — no cosmetic
  rewrite was performed. The provenance checker records them as class-C findings so the variation is
  documented rather than silently "fixed".

Result: exactly one unambiguous machine-readable state per TV entry; the remaining CSV/JSON variation
is a documented vocabulary equivalence.
