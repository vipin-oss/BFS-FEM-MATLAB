\section{High-Wavenumber Phase Velocity Asymptotics}
\label{app:asymptotics}

This appendix provides the formal mathematical proof that strain-gradient elasticity without micro-inertia ($\ell_{\mathrm{i}} = 0$) results in an unbounded phase velocity as wavenumber $k \to \infty$, whereas the inclusion of micro-inertia ($\ell_{\mathrm{i}} > 0$) strictly bounds the wave speed to a finite asymptotic horizon.

Consider one-dimensional transverse shear wave propagation in a Mindlin Form-II gradient-elastic continuum governed by the equation of motion:
\begin{equation}
\mu \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} - \mu l^2 \frac{\partial^4 u}{\partial x^4} = \rho \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} - \rho \ell_{\mathrm{i}}^2 \frac{\partial^4 u}{\partial x^2 \partial t^2},
\label{eq:appA_pde}
\end{equation}
where $\mu$ is the shear modulus, $\rho$ is the mass density, $l$ is the characteristic gradient-stiffness length, and $\ell_{\mathrm{i}}$ is the micro-inertia length parameter. Substituting the time-harmonic plane wave ansatz $u(x, t) = A e^{\iu(k x - \omega t)}$ into Eq.~\eqref{eq:appA_pde} yields:
\begin{equation}
-\mu k^2 - \mu l^2 k^4 = -\rho \omega^2 - \rho \ell_{\mathrm{i}}^2 \omega^2 k^2.
\label{eq:appA_char}
\end{equation}
Rearranging terms provides the exact analytical dispersion relation:
\begin{equation}
\omega^2(k) = \frac{\mu}{\rho} \frac{k^2 \left( 1 + l^2 k^2 \right)}{1 + \ell_{\mathrm{i}}^2 k^2}.
\label{eq:appA_dispersion}
\end{equation}
The phase velocity $v_p(k) \equiv \omega(k)/k$ is therefore:
\begin{equation}
v_p(k) = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\rho}} \sqrt{\frac{1 + l^2 k^2}{1 + \ell_{\mathrm{i}}^2 k^2}} = c_T \sqrt{\frac{1 + l^2 k^2}{1 + \ell_{\mathrm{i}}^2 k^2}},
\label{eq:appA_vp}
\end{equation}
where $c_T = \sqrt{\mu/\rho}$ is the classical acoustic shear wave speed.

\paragraph{Case 1: Formulation without Micro-Inertia ($\ell_{\mathrm{i}} = 0$).}
Setting $\ell_{\mathrm{i}} = 0$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:appA_vp} yields:
\begin{equation}
v_p(k) = c_T \sqrt{1 + l^2 k^2}.
\label{eq:appA_vp_zero}
\end{equation}
In the short-wave limit ($k \to \infty$), the phase velocity behaves asymptotically as:
\begin{equation}
\lim_{k \to \infty} v_p(k) = \lim_{k \to \infty} c_T l k \to \infty.
\label{eq:appA_unbounded}
\end{equation}
Similarly, the group velocity $v_g(k) = \dif\omega/\dif k$ is given by:
\begin{equation}
v_g(k) = c_T \frac{1 + 2 l^2 k^2}{\sqrt{1 + l^2 k^2}} \sim 2 c_T l k \to \infty \quad \text{as } k \to \infty.
\label{eq:appA_vg_unbounded}
\end{equation}
Consequently, higher-order stiffness without micro-inertia leads to unbounded propagation speeds at high frequencies, violating relativistic causality and physical admissibility.

\paragraph{Case 2: Formulation with Micro-Inertia ($\ell_{\mathrm{i}} > 0$).}
When $\ell_{\mathrm{i}} > 0$, expanding Eq.~\eqref{eq:appA_vp} as $k \to \infty$ produces:
\begin{equation}
v_{p,\infty} \equiv \lim_{k \to \infty} v_p(k) = c_T \lim_{k \to \infty} \sqrt{\frac{l^2 k^2 (1 + \frac{1}{l^2 k^2})}{\ell_{\mathrm{i}}^2 k^2 (1 + \frac{1}{\ell_{\mathrm{i}}^2 k^2})}} = c_T \frac{l}{\ell_{\mathrm{i}}}.
\label{eq:appA_vp_bounded}
\end{equation}
Non-dimensionalizing by $c_T = 1.0$ yields the finite asymptotic horizon:
\begin{equation}
\bar{v}_{T,\infty} = \frac{\bar{l}}{\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{i}}}.
\label{eq:appA_v_horizon}
\end{equation}
For our baseline Case H parameters ($l_2 = 0.10$, $\ell_{\mathrm{i}} = 0.3162277$), the transverse asymptotic horizon is:
\begin{equation}
\bar{v}_{T,\infty} = \frac{0.10}{0.3162277} \approx 0.3162.
\label{eq:appA_horizon_val}
\end{equation}
As verified numerically in Section~\ref{sec:steering}, the finite element phase velocity at $\bar{k} = 200$ attains $\bar{v}_p = 0.3163$, matching the analytical horizon within $0.03\%$. Micro-inertia is thus a constitutive necessity that guarantees bounded wave speeds across all scales.
