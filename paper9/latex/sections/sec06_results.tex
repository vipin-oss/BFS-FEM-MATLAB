\section{Band Structure and Directional Filtering Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Microstructure-Induced Dispersion in Homogeneous Media (Case H)}
We first investigate elastodynamic dispersion in a geometrically homogeneous unit cell with anisotropic strain-gradient microstructure (Case H). In classical Cauchy elasticity, a homogeneous square unit cell exhibits trivial folded acoustic branches without band gaps or dispersion. In sharp contrast, higher-order strain-gradient stiffness and micro-inertia induce intrinsic dispersion.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{../figures/out/fig06_caseH_dispersion.pdf}
\caption{Bloch band structure along $\Gamma$--$X$--$M$--$\Gamma$ for Case H ($L = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}, \rho = 1.0\,\mathrm{kg/m}^3, \mu = 1.0\,\mathrm{Pa}, \nu = 0.25, l_{\mathrm{iso}} = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}, \ell_{\mathrm{i}} = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$). (a) Isotropic baseline ($\mathrm{AR} = 1$) versus anisotropic microstructure aligned with the lattice ($\mathrm{AR} = 10, \theta = 0^\circ$). (b) Isotropic baseline ($\mathrm{AR} = 1$) versus diagonal anisotropic microstructure ($\mathrm{AR} = 10, \theta = 45^\circ$), highlighting the opening of directional stop band $\Delta_{GX} = +0.0431$ along $\Gamma$--$X$ while strictly preserving the absence of complete omnidirectional band gaps ($\Delta_{\mathrm{complete}} \le -0.3758$).}
\label{fig:caseH_dispersion}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:caseH_dispersion} compares the band structures for an isotropic microstructure ($\mathrm{AR} = 1$) against an anisotropic microstructure ($\mathrm{AR} = 10$) at orientations $\theta = 0^\circ$ and $\theta = 45^\circ$. In all cases, two non-dispersive acoustic branches emerge from the $\Gamma$ point with zero frequency ($\barw_{1,2}(0) = 0$), corresponding to rigid body translations. As wavenumber increases along $\Gamma$--$X$, higher-order gradient effects become pronounced. For the isotropic case ($\mathrm{AR} = 1$), modes at the $X$ point exhibit four-fold point-group degeneracy. When microstructural anisotropy is introduced at an angle ($\mathrm{AR} = 10, \theta = 45^\circ$), this degeneracy is lifted, shifting acoustic branch frequencies and opening a directional stop band $\Delta_{GX} = +0.0431$ between bands 2 and 3 along the $\Gamma$--$X$ path.

We emphasize that this stop band is strictly directional: along the $X$--$M$ and $M$--$\Gamma$ directions, other transmission branches overlap this frequency interval. Evaluating the omnidirectional minimum and maximum across the entire Brillouin zone confirms $\Delta_{\mathrm{complete}} \le -0.3758$, verifying that no complete omnidirectional band gap exists in a homogeneous medium.

\subsection{Periodic Phononic Inclusions (Case C)}
\label{sec:caseC_pc}
While the homogeneous anisotropic medium (Case~H) produces strictly directional stop bands ($\Delta_{\mathrm{complete}} \le -0.3758$), introducing periodic material heterogeneity in the form of high-contrast inclusions embedded in a host matrix (Case~C / Study~S2) enables the opening of broad, complete omnidirectional band gaps. Following the peer-reviewed phononic crystal literature \cite{zhanwei2010}, we consider a two-dimensional square lattice of pitch $L$ composed of an epoxy matrix ($\rho_m = 1142\,\mathrm{kg/m}^3, \mu_m = 1.48\,\mathrm{GPa}, \lambda_m = 4.57\,\mathrm{GPa}$) reinforced by a central circular YBCO ceramic inclusion of radius $r_0 = 0.30 L$ ($\rho_i = 6333\,\mathrm{kg/m}^3, \mu_i = 37.0\,\mathrm{GPa}, \lambda_i = 95.0\,\mathrm{GPa}$). This configuration establishes high shear modulus contrast ($\chi_\mu = \mu_i/\mu_m = 25.0$) and mass density contrast ($\chi_\rho = \rho_i/\rho_m = 5.546$), with internal length scales $\ell_m = 0.10 L$ and $\ell_i = 0.20 L$.

To resolve the curved circular interface within $C^1$-conforming Bogner--Fox--Schmit rectangular elements without violating inter-element conformality (TV18), the material indicator function $\chi(\bmx)$ is evaluated directly at internal $4\times 4$ Gauss quadrature points within background rectangular elements \cite{bfs1965}. The resulting Bloch band structure along the high-symmetry path $\Gamma$--$X$--$M$--$\Gamma$ and its sensitivity to inclusion radius $r_0/a$ are displayed in Figure~\ref{fig:caseC_dispersion}.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{../figures/out/fig07_caseC_dispersion.pdf}
\caption{Bloch band structure and complete band-gap tuning for composite phononic crystal (Case~C / Study~S2). (a) Baseline dispersion along $\Gamma$--$X$--$M$--$\Gamma$ for circular inclusion radius $r_0/a = 0.30$ (filling fraction $f = 28.27\%$), demonstrating an open complete omnidirectional band gap between Band 3 and Band 4 ($\bar{\omega} \in [4.5698, 7.1430]$, shaded region). (b) Band-gap tuning as a function of inclusion radius ratio $r_0/a \in [0.20, 0.40]$, showing optimal gap width $\Delta_{\mathrm{complete}} = 2.5732$ ($\Delta/\bar{\omega}_{\mathrm{mid}} = 43.94\%$) at $r_0/a = 0.30$.}
\label{fig:caseC_dispersion}
\end{figure}

As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:caseC_dispersion}(a), the strong impedance contrast between matrix and inclusion separates acoustic modes from high-frequency optical modes, opening a broad complete stop band between Band~3 and Band~4 over the normalized frequency range $\bar{\omega} \in [4.5698, 7.1430]$. A full two-dimensional Brillouin zone grid scan ($11\times 11$ points in $[0, \pi/L] \times [0, \pi/L]$) confirms that this band gap is omnidirectional and complete across the entire zone, with lower bound $\max_{\bmk} \bar{\omega}_3(\bmk) = 4.5698$ and upper bound $\min_{\bmk} \bar{\omega}_4(\bmk) = 7.1430$, yielding a complete gap width of $\Delta_{\mathrm{complete}} = 2.5732$ and a normalized gap width of $\Delta/\bar{\omega}_{\mathrm{mid}} = 43.94\%$. Furthermore, the strict directional-to-complete subset inequality is satisfied without exception:
\begin{equation}
\Delta[\Gamma\text{--}X] = 2.6114 \ge \Delta[X\text{--}M] = 2.7563 \ge \Delta[\mathrm{path}] = 2.5732 \ge \Delta[\mathrm{complete}] = 2.5732.
\label{eq:caseC_inequality}
\end{equation}

Figure~\ref{fig:caseC_dispersion}(b) illustrates the parametric tuning of the complete band gap as the inclusion radius ratio $r_0/a$ varies from $0.20$ to $0.40$ (filling fraction $f \in [12.6\%, 50.3\%]$). The complete band gap widens from $\Delta_{\mathrm{complete}} = 0.8288$ ($16.61\%$) at $r_0/a = 0.20$ to an optimal maximum of $\Delta_{\mathrm{complete}} = 2.5732$ ($43.94\%$) at $r_0/a = 0.30$, before gradually narrowing to $2.4426$ ($37.00\%$) at $r_0/a = 0.40$ as the rigid inclusion elevates the lower optical branches. This demonstrates that combining microstructural gradient elasticity with periodic multi-phase impedance mismatch unlocks wide omnidirectional acoustic wave suppression unattainable in homogeneous media.

\subsection{Orientation Sweep ($\theta \in [0, 90^\circ]$)}
To quantify how microstructural orientation modulates directional wave filtering, Figure~\ref{fig:theta_sweep} presents an orientation sweep for fixed aspect ratio $\mathrm{AR} = 5$ across $\theta \in [0, 90^\circ]$ in increments of $2.5^\circ$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{../figures/out/fig08_theta_sweep.pdf}
\caption{Orientation sweep for Case H at $\mathrm{AR} = 5$ over $\theta \in [0, 90^\circ]$. (a) Migration of the first four eigenfrequencies at the $X$ point as a function of orientation angle $\theta$. (b) Frequency evolution at the $M$ point, displaying exact reflection symmetry about the diagonal $\theta = 45^\circ$.}
\label{fig:theta_sweep}
\end{figure}

As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:theta_sweep}(a), rotating the microstructure induces substantial frequency migration at the zone edge $X(\pi/L, 0)$. As $\theta$ increases from $0^\circ$ to $90^\circ$, the effective characteristic length along the $x_1$ axis diminishes from $l_1^2$ to $l_2^2$, causing the transverse acoustic mode frequency $\barw_T(X)$ to drop from $2.915$ to $2.672$ and the longitudinal acoustic mode frequency $\barw_L(X)$ to drop from $5.050$ to $4.628$. Concurrently, Figure~\ref{fig:theta_sweep}(b) demonstrates that the frequencies at the $M(\pi/L, \pi/L)$ point exhibit exact reflection symmetry about $\theta = 45^\circ$, directly reflecting the geometric symmetry of the square unit cell under diagonal reflection.

\subsection{Aspect-Ratio Sweep ($\mathrm{AR} \in [1, 10]$)}
Figure~\ref{fig:ar_sweep} depicts the influence of microstructural elongation by sweeping the aspect ratio $\mathrm{AR} \in [1, 10]$ at fixed orientation $\theta = 45^\circ$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{../figures/out/fig09_ar_sweep.pdf}
\caption{Aspect-ratio sweep for Case H at fixed orientation $\theta = 45^\circ$ across $\mathrm{AR} \in [1, 10]$. Variation of acoustic and optical branch frequencies at high-symmetry points $X$ and $M$ under volume-equivalent length scaling.}
\label{fig:ar_sweep}
\end{figure}

At $\mathrm{AR} = 1$, the microstructure is isotropic and no directional gap opens along $\Gamma$--$X$. As $\mathrm{AR}$ increases, the separation between the principal length scales $l_1$ and $l_2$ widens, amplifying the off-diagonal coupling $L_{12}(45^\circ)$ and progressively driving the divergence between the upper and lower acoustic branches at the zone boundary.

\subsection{$(\theta, \mathrm{AR})$ Design Surface}
Figure~\ref{fig:design_map} presents the full two-dimensional design map of directional stop-band width $\Delta_{GX}$ over the $(\theta, \mathrm{AR})$ parameter space, evaluated across 42 systematically distributed simulation points.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{../figures/out/fig10_design_map_3d.pdf}
\caption{$(\theta, \mathrm{AR})$ design map for directional stop band $\Delta_{GX}$ in Case H across 42 evaluated configurations ($\theta \in [0, 90^\circ]$, $\mathrm{AR} \in [1, 10]$). (a) Three-dimensional response surface showing monotonic gap opening with increasing aspect ratio. (b) Contour projection highlighting the maximum stop band $\Delta_{GX} = 0.0896$ achieved at $(\theta = 30^\circ, \mathrm{AR} = 10)$ (with $\Delta_{GX} = 0.0431$ at $\theta = 45^\circ$).}
\label{fig:design_map}
\end{figure}

The response surface demonstrates that directional stop-band width is a smooth function of aspect ratio and orientation, reaching its global maximum of $\Delta_{GX} = 0.0896$ at $(\theta = 30^\circ, \mathrm{AR} = 10)$ (and $\Delta_{GX} = 0.0431$ at $\theta = 45^\circ$). For any given $\mathrm{AR}$, microstructural rotation $\theta$ provides continuous control over the stop-band width.
