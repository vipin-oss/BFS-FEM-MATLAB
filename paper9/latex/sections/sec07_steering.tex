\section{Anisotropic Wave Steering and Physical Admissibility}
\label{sec:steering}

\subsection{Wave-Vector Steering and Group Velocity Deviation}
To examine energy redirection, we analyze the propagation of elastic waves along a constant wavenumber contour $|\bmk| = \bar{k} = 0.5$ parameterized by propagation angle $\phi \in [0, 2\pi)$. In an isotropic elastic medium, energy travels collinear with the wave vector ($\bmv_g \parallel \bmk$), yielding zero angular deviation ($\delta = \angle\bmv_g - \angle\bmk = 0$). When microstructural anisotropy is introduced, the group velocity deviates from the phase vector.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{../figures/out/fig12_ifc_wave_steering.pdf}
\caption{Anisotropic wave steering and group velocity characteristics along the circular wave-vector contour $|\bmk| = \bar{k} = 0.5$ at $\theta = 45^\circ$. (a) Angular steering deviation $\delta(\phi) = \angle\bmv_g - \angle\bmk$ for aspect ratios $\mathrm{AR} \in \{1, 5, 10\}$, showing peak steering angles $\delta_{\max} = 0.00^\circ$ ($\mathrm{AR}=1$), $1.41^\circ$ ($\mathrm{AR}=5$), and $2.78^\circ$ ($\mathrm{AR}=10$). (b) Group velocity magnitude $|\bmv_g|(\phi)$ displaying strong four-fold directional modulation.}
\label{fig:ifc_wave_steering}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:ifc_wave_steering}(a) plots the steering deviation angle $\delta(\phi)$ as a function of wave-vector angle $\phi$ for $\mathrm{AR} \in \{1, 5, 10\}$ at fixed microstructural orientation $\theta = 45^\circ$. For the isotropic medium ($\mathrm{AR} = 1$), the steering deviation vanishes ($\delta_{\max} = 0.00^\circ$, well within numerical noise). As the microstructural aspect ratio increases to $\mathrm{AR} = 5$ and $\mathrm{AR} = 10$, significant wave steering develops, with maximum deviation angles reaching $\delta_{\max} = 1.41^\circ$ and $\delta_{\max} = 2.78^\circ$, respectively. Concurrently, Figure~\ref{fig:ifc_wave_steering}(b) reveals that the group velocity magnitude $|\bmv_g|(\phi)$ undergoes pronounced directional modulation, demonstrating that microstructural orientation acts as an active acoustic steering mechanism.

\subsection{Orientation Sweep of the Steering Pattern and Figure of Merit}
To quantify orientation control of the steering pattern, the constant-contour analysis is extended to the full orientation grid $\theta \in \{0^\circ, 15^\circ, 30^\circ, 45^\circ, 60^\circ, 75^\circ, 90^\circ\}$ at $\mathrm{AR} \in \{5, 10\}$ on the same ring $|\bmk| = \bar{k} = 0.5$, using the identical acoustic branch, sampling, and central-difference group-velocity evaluation as above.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{../figures/out/fig14_s7_steering_sweep.pdf}
\caption{Orientation dependence of the steering pattern on the ring $|\bmk| = 0.5$. (a) Maximum group-velocity deviation $\delta_{\max}(\theta)$ (filled markers, left axis) and principal steering axis $\phi^*(\theta) = (\arg\max_{\phi} \delta(\phi)) \bmod 90^\circ$ (open markers, right axis) for $\mathrm{AR} \in \{5, 10\}$: $\delta_{\max}$ is exactly symmetric under $\theta \to 90^\circ - \theta$ (verified to $10^{-7}\,$deg), and $\phi^*$ tracks $(45^\circ-\theta) \bmod 90^\circ$ on the locked sampling grid (excursions of at most $5^\circ$ at $\mathrm{AR}=10$). The steering figure of merit on the locked $5^\circ$ sampling grid is $M_s = \phi^*(90^\circ) - \phi^*(0^\circ) = 0^\circ$ ($\mathrm{AR}=5$) and $10^\circ$ ($\mathrm{AR}=10$). (b) Ring deviation field $\delta(\phi;\ \theta=0^\circ)$ (solid) against the mirrored field $\delta(90^\circ-\phi;\ \theta=90^\circ)$ (open markers) at $\mathrm{AR}=10$, confirming the exact mirror co-symmetry $\delta(\phi;\theta) = \delta(90^\circ-\phi;\,90^\circ-\theta)$.}

\end{figure}

Table~\ref{tab:steering_sweep} and Figure~\ref{fig:s7_steering_sweep} report the computed results. The deviation maximum is orientation independent across the full sweep to within $10^{-5}\,$deg: $\delta_{\max} = 1.4146^\circ$ at $\mathrm{AR}=5$ and $2.7846^\circ$ at $\mathrm{AR}=10$ at every $\theta$ (relative variation below $5\times10^{-4}\%$). The deviation-maximum direction follows $\phi^*(\theta) = (45^\circ - \theta) \bmod 90^\circ$ on the locked grid (with excursions of at most $5^\circ$ at $\mathrm{AR}=10$), i.e., the steering lobes continuously counter-rotate with the microstructure inside each $90^\circ$ sector.

Defining the steering figure of merit
\begin{equation}
M_s(\mathrm{AR}) \;=\; \phi^*(90^\circ,\,\mathrm{AR}) - \phi^*(0^\circ,\,\mathrm{AR}),
\label{eq:Ms_def}
\end{equation}
the evaluation yields
\begin{equation}
M_s(\mathrm{AR}=5) = 0^\circ \quad (\phi^*(90^\circ) = \phi^*(0^\circ) = 45^\circ), \qquad M_s(\mathrm{AR}=10) = 10^\circ \quad (\phi^*(0^\circ) = 40^\circ,\ \phi^*(90^\circ) = 50^\circ),
\label{eq:Ms_value}
\end{equation}
The residual steering figure of merit is bounded by the $5^\circ$ sampling step of the ring sweep, and its value is set by the two field symmetries verified numerically over the entire sweep (maximum residuals $1.3\times10^{-6}\,$deg for both): the headless periodicity $\delta(\phi + 180^\circ;\,\theta) = \delta(\phi;\,\theta)$ and the mirror co-symmetry $\delta(\phi;\,\theta) = \delta(90^\circ - \phi;\, 90^\circ - \theta)$, the latter being the group-velocity counterpart of the rotation--swap symmetry of the length-scale tensor established in Section~\ref{sec:continuum} (Eq.~\eqref{eq:L_components}). Because the $\theta = 90^\circ$ deviation field is the mirror of the $\theta = 0^\circ$ field, the unsigned principal steering axis (mod $90^\circ$) of the rotated microstructure is the mirror image (mod $90^\circ$) of its unrotated counterpart, and $\delta_{\max}$ is invariant under $\theta \to 90^\circ - \theta$ to within $10^{-7}\,$deg. Orientation tuning therefore repositions the steering lobes within the fundamental sector---following $(45^\circ-\theta) \bmod 90^\circ$---while the attainable deviation amplitude is invariant over all orientations to within $10^{-5}\,$deg (relative variation below $5\times10^{-4}\%$).

\subsection{Energy Partition and Double-Stress Work}
In Mindlin Form-II gradient elasticity, total strain energy $W$ is composed of classical energy $W_c = \frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega} \sigma_{ij}\varepsilon_{ij}\,\dif\Omega$ and gradient energy $W_g = \frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega} \tau_{ijk}\eta_{ijk}\,\dif\Omega$. Figure~\ref{fig:energy_microinertia}(a) plots the gradient energy partition fraction $W_g/W$ as a function of non-dimensional wavenumber $\bar{k}$ along the $\Gamma$--$X$ direction.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{../figures/out/fig13_energy_microinertia.pdf}
\caption{(a) Evolution of the gradient strain energy partition fraction $W_g/W$ versus wavenumber $\bar{k}$, demonstrating a monotonic increase from $0.20\%$ at $\bar{k} = 0.1$ to $16.49\%$ at $\bar{k} = 1.0$. (b) Verification of micro-inertia high-wavenumber phase velocity asymptotics: with micro-inertia ($\ell_{\mathrm{i}} > 0$), phase velocity is strictly bounded, attaining $\bar{v}_p = 0.3163$ at $\bar{k} = 200$ (matching the theoretical horizon $v_{T,\infty} = 0.3162$ within $0.03\%$); without micro-inertia ($\ell_{\mathrm{i}} = 0$), phase velocity diverges unbounded ($\bar{v}_p = 39.75$ at $\bar{k} = 200$), proving physical inadmissibility.}
\label{fig:energy_microinertia}
\end{figure}

At long wavelengths ($\bar{k} = 0.1$), gradient strain energy constitutes a negligible fraction ($W_g/W = 0.20\%$), indicating that classical elasticity dominates macroscopic deformation. However, as the wavelength approaches the unit cell dimensions ($\bar{k} \to 1.0$), double-stress work increases monotonically, accounting for $16.49\%$ of the total strain energy. This substantial energetic contribution underscores the necessity of higher-order gradient formulations in short-wave regimes.

\subsection{Micro-Inertia and High-Wavenumber Admissibility}
Figure~\ref{fig:energy_microinertia}(b) provides numerical confirmation of the asymptotic analysis derived in Appendix~\ref{app:asymptotics}. For the formulation lacking micro-inertia ($\ell_{\mathrm{i}} = 0$), the computed phase velocity grows without bound with increasing wavenumber, reaching $\bar{v}_p = 39.75$ at $\bar{k} = 200$. In sharp contrast, incorporating micro-inertia ($\ell_{\mathrm{i}} > 0$) regularizes the high-frequency spectrum, causing the phase velocity to asymptote to a strictly finite horizon:
\begin{equation}
\bar{v}_{T,\infty} = \frac{\bar{l}_{\mathrm{eff}}}{\sqrt{10}\,\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{i}}} = \frac{0.20}{\sqrt{10} \times 0.20} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{10}} \approx 0.3162.
\label{eq:v_horizon_sec7}
\end{equation}
At $\bar{k} = 200$, the finite element model yields $\bar{v}_p = 0.3163$, in remarkable agreement with the theoretical horizon (relative difference $0.03\%$). This confirms the constitutive role of micro-inertia in ensuring bounded wave speeds and causality.
